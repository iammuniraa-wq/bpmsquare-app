# gcc_tender
status: experimental
plain: GCC tender deals. The Pipeline gets a Tenders tab for bids the issuer invites: each tender is a deal with its own stages, a bid end date countdown, a recorded go or no-go decision, a bid guarantee block and reminders to the owner and team before the bid ends. A customer email can become a tender in one tap.
rules:
- A tender is an ordinary deal plus a tender block; the Deals tab and board never show it, and the Tenders tab never shows a standard deal.
- Tender stages are Received, Bid/No-bid, Estimating, Submitted, Clarifications, then Awarded, Lost or No-bid, which close the deal as won, lost or dropped.
- A team member proposes go or no-go with a reason and a manager approves or rejects it with a comment; the tender cannot leave Bid/No-bid for Estimating without an approved go, nor go to No-bid without an approved no-go, and the system records who and when; an approved go moves the deal to Estimating and an approved no-go to No-bid by itself.
- Approvers are chosen in Settings, Tenders (workspace admins by default); the proposer cannot approve their own proposal unless the workspace allows it.
- A bid guarantee moves through Requested, Issued, Submitted and Released; releasing it is a confirmed step, and a released guarantee stops the expiry warning and the guarantee line in reminders.
- Request supplier prices raises an RFQ from the deal's rows, carrying the client's item numbers, to any number of suppliers (vendors or subcontractors can be filtered); the replies are compared and awarded on the Costing tab.
- The owner and team are emailed 7, 3 and 1 days before the bid end date and on the day, once each, until the bid is submitted.
- The Bill of quantities reads the client's Excel (.xlsx) into deal lines, keeping their item numbers and cost codes; a revised file updates lines by item number and never silently removes a priced line.
- The Bill of quantities is one table that replaces the old BOQ and Lines tabs: one row per client line, the client's item number always shown and never renumbered, with the source of the rate and chips for the estimate and the RFQ it is priced from, Unpriced and Stale; filters All, Unpriced and Stale; a totals strip of lines, priced percent and the total at cost plus markup; long BOQs are paged and a phone gets cards.
- Each BOQ line is priced from the client's own rate, a product BOM or estimate, a past awarded tender, a supplier quote or a manual rate, and shows which source set it and how old it is.
- A row opens a side panel (a bottom sheet on a phone) to change its quantity, rate and description; a rate typed there is saved as Manual and keeps the source it replaced. Where the cost came from (the estimate, the RFQ it was awarded under) is shown and cannot be changed there: a supplier is awarded only on the Costing compare screen.
- Price all lines uses the deal's markup on cost, 15% unless the workspace sets another in config.tender.default_markup_pct; the toolbar has Upload BOQ, Match to products and Price all, and no downloads.
- A supplier or past-project price older than the workspace's price validity (180 days by default, set in Settings, Tenders) is marked stale and is never applied automatically; a person may still choose it knowingly.
- Download priced BOQ (offered on the Bid tab, not on the Bill of quantities toolbar) writes the unit rate and amount into the client's own file and keeps its layout; Export answer sheet gives a plain Excel list for a portal form such as Ariba. Cost and margin never appear in either file.
- A tender deal has six tabs, in this order: Overview, Bill of quantities, Costing, Bid, Room and Competitors. There is no Lines, Estimates, Quotes, Related or Activity tab; an old link to one lands on the nearest tab, and History is in the header menu.
- The one primary button in a tender's header follows its stage: Propose bid decision when it is Received, Submit bid in Estimating (Revise bid once a version has been submitted), Record the outcome when it is Submitted or in Clarifications, and Create contract project (or Open contract project) when it is Awarded and won. A tender header never offers Convert to Standard Quote, and marking a tender Won offers Create contract project now, never a quote.
- The Bid tab opens with a readiness line that warns about lines without a price, stale prices, no approved go and a guarantee not yet marked Submitted, and never blocks; then Submit bid or Revise bid, the numbered versions, a comparison of any two, and Documents we send.
- The Bid tab saves each bid that goes in as a numbered version (no limit) with the deal's lines and total as they were, never a cost or markup; from the second version a reason is required, and any two versions can be compared line by line and by total.
- Submit bid moves the tender to Submitted and Revise bid returns it to Estimating; a saved version is never edited or deleted.
- Documents we send on the Bid tab holds the priced BOQ, the answer sheet and Make a Standard Quote from this version: the quote is made from the version you pick, keeps the client's own item numbers, links back to the tender and is listed there with the other quotes made from it.
- History, in the header menu, opens the changes to the tender and its logged calls in a side panel (a bottom sheet on a phone).
- On a phone the header is the title, the stage, the primary button and the menu, with the other facts folded into Deal facts; the tabs scroll sideways and a selected tab opens with its content in view.
- A tender whose lines were typed by hand, with no client file uploaded, shows those lines as rows of the Bill of quantities and can add, change or delete a line there.
- Log a call records a call, meeting or site visit with a next step and date (from the Deal Room), and the calls are listed in History; an overdue next step shows in Open work on the Overview, which opens History, until it is ticked done.
- Once a tender is Awarded, Lost or No-bid, the Overview records the outcome: awarded value, who won and the winning price, and why it was lost (the reason and the winner are required for Lost), with our last submitted total beside the price.
- The Costing tab lists the estimates made for the tender and starts a new one from a template; the estimate keeps the tender it belongs to and opens in the ordinary estimator with a link back.
- A Bill of quantities row shows the estimate that prices it as a chip and in its panel; a line is priced from the whole estimate, one of its lines or a WBS item, spread over the BOQ quantity or taken per unit, with a share, and the server works the cost out from the estimate itself, and the cost never reaches the priced Excel, the answer sheet or a bid version.
- The Costing tab lists the estimates and the RFQs of the tender together, each with the BOQ rows it prices, its state, its total and its age, with four figures above (rows priced, rows that still need a cost, RFQs out, replies in); a new estimate starts from a template and keeps the tender it belongs to, and one New RFQ form asks any number of suppliers about chosen BOQ rows.
- On the Costing compare screen the suppliers sit side by side for each line with the lowest valid price marked; Award and price stores the supplier's price as the row's cost, adds the markup (15 percent unless Settings, Tenders or the person says otherwise, so cost 100 gives a rate of 115) and sets the price source to the RFQ, for example RFQ-0012, awarded. A tender's RFQ is awarded only there, a price that is not the lowest valid one needs a reason, and the cost and markup never reach the priced Excel, the answer sheet or a bid version.
- In a Bill of quantities row's panel a line can be priced from an estimate: the whole estimate, one of its lines or a WBS item, spread over the BOQ quantity or taken per unit, with a share; the server works the cost out from the estimate itself, and the cost never reaches the priced Excel, the answer sheet or a bid version.
- Build up this rate, in the row panel, starts an estimate from a template with the line's quantity filled in, and the line is priced from its total divided by the quantity when the person returns; on the Costing tab, Price the BOQ rows (Allocate to BOQ) spreads a whole-scope estimate over the lines it covers and shows what is not yet allocated. The Costing line that says how many rows still need a cost opens the Bill of quantities already filtered to them.
- A BOQ line priced from an estimate shows Estimate changed, re-price once the estimate is saved again; a bid that is already in is never re-priced by itself.
- Raise RFQ for missing costs asks suppliers (any number, no minimum) about the estimate lines with no cost or a cost older than the RFQ price validity; these RFQs also show on the Costing tab and on the estimate, and awarding a line there writes the supplier's cost and date into the estimate line and re-prices the BOQ lines priced from it for review.
- A contract project made from the won tender takes the cost lines of the estimates that priced the BOQ, by resource type, with the BOQ cost codes and the estimate's real costs; lines without an estimate keep the margin rule.
- The Competitors tab lists the companies the team expects to bid against, each with a website, country, whether they are expected to price below, similar to or above us, strengths and a note; one can be marked as the winner.
- Company insights on a competitor runs the company analysis for it only when pressed, using part of the AI allowance, and shows a saved card again without cost; Analyse all asks first and says how many analyses it will run.
- Who won, in the close dialog and the Outcome section, is picked from the tender's competitors or added as someone else, and the winner is saved on the deal as before.

- The Overview of a tender deal is one tab: what the client asked for, Documents (the source email, its files and the Change requests count), Open work and the Bid guarantee; the header of every tab carries the bid decision marker, the Bid end date countdown and the guarantee state.
- Submit bid and Revise bid warn, and never block, about lines without a price, stale prices, no approved go and a guarantee not yet marked Submitted.
- When a bid is submitted, the internal cost of each line and its markup are kept privately with that bid version; they never appear in a bid version's lines, a priced Excel, an answer sheet, a Standard Quote or the API.
- The contract project made from the won tender starts its budget from the winning bid version's recorded cost, not from the BOQ as it stands later; a bid submitted before costs were kept falls back to the current BOQ and the budget note says so.
- A contract project made from a tender has a Buy-out tab after Budget: it groups budget lines into packages, asks the client's approved suppliers for one price per package in numbered rounds that are all kept for audit, shows the quotes side by side with the lowest valid price marked, awards a package to one supplier (a reason is needed when it is not the lowest valid price) and un-awards it with a reason until the handover is sent. Purchase orders stay in ePROMIS or the ERP.
- Buy-out shows four figures (budget in packages, committed, best quotes against budget, packages needing a decision), filters by status with counts, and a Download Excel and Mark as sent to ePROMIS handover per awarded package; committed over the budget is a warning, never a block. The Budget tab shows a Committed column for lines in an awarded package and the Overview a Buy-out progress card.
- Suppliers has a Groups view where a group of approved suppliers is named, linked to a client account and given its members; for a deal or contract project of that client the Costing RFQ form and the Buy-out request offer only the group's suppliers, with a note saying so, and a client with no group can be asked any supplier.
- The flag is set by a platform admin; a workspace without it sees no new tab, menu entry or screen, and standard deals behave exactly as before.
- A client can have an approved supplier list (a supplier group linked to its account): a tender deal, its estimates' RFQs and the project's buy-out rounds then offer only those suppliers, and the server refuses anyone else naming them; a client without a list leaves every supplier open, and standard RFQs are never limited.
