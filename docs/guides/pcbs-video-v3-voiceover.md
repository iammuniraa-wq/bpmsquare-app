**S1: Menu tour (Spaces)**
Everything is grouped into spaces: Home, Sales, Service, Marketing, Master data, Workforce, Insights and Admin. Open a space and its menu opens beside it.

**M1: Newsletter in English and Arabic**
The Newsletter Studio drafts the email, and each language sits in its own tab. In the Arabic tab, every translated line waits for a person to check it. Compare languages shows the Arabic and the English side by side, and the phone preview shows how it will look in your customer's hand.

**M2: Journey**
A journey is a simple follow-up you set once. Here, three days after a quote is sent, the customer gets a newsletter, and the account owner gets a task to call. Consent is checked again at every send, and the screen says in plain words when a message will be held.

**M3: Account-based programs**
For the companies that matter most, an account-based program tracks each target account through its buying stage, with the buying group behind it: decision maker, influencer and user. Gaps are shown, so your team knows who still needs to be reached.

**F1: Fence projects**
The same system handles any kind of project. In the Fence Design Studio, set the length, posts, mesh and gates, and see the fence in two and three dimensions. The quantities list builds itself, and one click continues to a quote.

**C1: Coverage (instead of a fixed sales area)**
In a traditional ERP, a sales area is a fixed combination of organisation, channel and division. Here, coverage is rule-based. A segment says which accounts, for example the south region or hospitals. A team says who looks after them. Coverage connects the two as owner, overlay or service, with a start date and a priority. It decides who owns an account, and which ERP endpoint a record is sent to, and it is recalculated live when the rules change.
